About AgroSense

Expert crop advice shouldn’t depend on where you farm.

AgroSense started with a simple question: what if every farmer in Ghana could ask an expert about a sick plant — and get an answer the same day?

Our story

Built in Accra, for the farms beyond it.

Ghana has millions of smallholder farms and roughly one extension officer for every 1,500 farmers. When a disease appears, the gap between noticing it and knowing what to do can cost a season.

AgroSense was built to close that gap. It began as a model trained to recognise plant disease from a photo, and grew into a platform: treatment plans written for Ghanaian conditions, weather advice for your crop, market prices across five markets, and SMS for farmers without smartphones.

We’re in beta, working towards field pilots with farmers and the people who support them.

How it was built

From a model to a platform.

Every stage, in the open.

{{ icon('github') }} View the source
Stage 1

The model

Two-phase transfer learning on MobileNetV2 across 54,305 PlantVillage images: 97.28% validation accuracy in a 2.7 MB file.

Stage 2

The backend

A FastAPI platform with PostgreSQL, Redis, secure accounts, and ten data models for farmers, crops, diagnoses, prices and more.

Stage 3

The diagnosis pipeline

Photo storage, preprocessing, inference and treatment advice tuned for Ghana — end to end in seconds.

Stage 4

Prices, weather and SMS

Five-market prices, a weather advisory, farm plots, an admin review loop and an SMS gateway for feature phones.

Stage 5

The mobile app

A React Native app for Android and iOS: scanning, results, history, weather, prices, Google sign-in.

Now

Hardened for the field

Graceful fallbacks for every service, a redesigned app, the crop guide, farm records, and preparation for field pilots.
