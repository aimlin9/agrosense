How it works

From one photo to a plan in under a minute.

Here’s exactly what happens between tapping “Take a photo” and reading your treatment plan — and what we do to make sure the answer can be trusted.

STEP 01

Choose the crop

Tomato, maize, pepper, potato, orange and nine more. Choosing first means the model only compares diseases that affect your crop. Crops it wasn’t trained on are marked “early access”, so you know to treat the result as a rough guide.

STEP 02

Take the photo

One affected leaf, in daylight, filling the frame. On the phone, AgroSense straightens the photo, shrinks it to a light JPEG and removes its location data — so it uploads quickly and privately.

STEP 03

The model looks closely

A MobileNetV2 network, fine-tuned on 54,305 leaf images, scores all 38 classes it knows, then narrows the answer to your crop. You see the top result, how confident it is, and the other possibilities.

STEP 04

You get a plan you can follow

Severity, what to do today, organic treatment first, chemical options sold in Ghana with safety notes, and prevention for next season. If our AI advice service is unavailable, a curated guide answers instead — you’re never left with nothing.

{{ section_head('Photo tips', 'Five habits for a reliable diagnosis.', 'Most uncertain results come from the photo, not the plant.') }}
{{ icon('sun') }}

Daylight, no flash

Shade the leaf from harsh midday sun if you can. Flash washes out colour.

{{ icon('target') }}

One leaf, filling the frame

Get close. A whole plant from far away gives the model too little to see.

{{ icon('eye') }}

Show the damage

Pick the leaf with clear spots or discolouration, not the healthiest one.

{{ icon('zap') }}

Hold still, in focus

Tap the screen to focus. Blurry photos lower confidence.

{{ icon('layers') }}

Plain background

Hold the leaf against your palm or the ground to separate it from others.
