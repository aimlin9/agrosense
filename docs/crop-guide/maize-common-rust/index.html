Crop guide/Maize/Common rust
MildFungusMaize

Common rust in maize

Puccinia sorghi

Produces rusty pustules on leaves. Usually a minor problem, but heavy early infections reduce yield.

SymptomsWhen it strikesHow it spreadsTreatmentPrevention
Caused byPuccinia sorghi (fungus)
SeverityMild

What you’ll see

  • Small, raised, cinnamon-brown pustules on both leaf surfaces
  • Pustules release rust-coloured powder when rubbed
  • Pustules turn dark brown to black later in the season
  • Yellowing of heavily infected leaves

When it strikes

Cool to moderate temperatures (16–25°C) with high humidity and dew.

How it spreads

Spores blow long distances on the wind.

What to do

In the field

  1. Plant resistant varieties
  2. Plant early to avoid peak spore periods
  3. Maintain balanced fertility

Organic — try first

Sulphur-based sprays can help on small plots.

Chemical — if it keeps spreading

Triazole or strobilurin fungicides, only if severe infection appears before tasselling.

Buy only from a certified agro-input dealer, follow the label exactly, wear gloves and a mask, and respect the waiting period before harvest. Ask your extension officer which product suits your farm.

Prevent it next season

  • Choose rust-resistant hybrids
  • Monitor leaves from knee height onwards
Not sure it’s common rust? Photograph the leaf with AgroSense — you’ll get the likely disease and how sure the model is. Get the app.

General guidance for smallholder farmers. Conditions vary — your local extension officer knows your farm best.
